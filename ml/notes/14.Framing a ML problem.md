### **Why Problem Framing Matters**

* **Career Growth:** Junior data scientists usually work on small, isolated tasks (such as data preprocessing or training a single model), whereas senior and lead data scientists must plan end-to-end solutions from scratch.
* **Cost Efficiency:** Jumping straight into coding without proper planning wastes time, engineering resources, and money if the chosen direction turns out to be wrong.

---

### **7-Step Framework for Framing an ML Problem (Netflix Case Study)**

1. **Convert the Business Problem into a Mathematical Problem**
* **Business Goal:** Increase company revenue.
* **Strategy:** Instead of spending heavily on acquiring new users or raising subscription prices, retain existing users who are likely to cancel.
* **Mathematical Goal:** Reduce the monthly **churn rate** (percentage of users leaving the platform) from a current 4% down to 3.75%.


2. **Identify the Type of ML Problem & End Product**
* **Define the Action (End Product):** Offer targeted discounts on the next month's subscription to at-risk customers to prevent them from leaving.
* **Classification vs. Regression:** Instead of simple binary classification ("will leave" vs. "won't leave"), frame it as a **regression problem** that predicts a **probability/churn score (0–100%)** for each user. Users with a higher probability of leaving receive larger discounts, optimizing promotional spending.


3. **Check for Existing Solutions**
* Consult leadership or other teams to see if similar models already exist internally (for example, an existing model forecasting overall company churn).
* Use existing work for inspiration on useful features and factors rather than starting completely from scratch.


4. **Identify Data Requirements & Sources**
* Brainstorm relevant behavioral features (columns) needed for the model, such as:
* Total watch time vs. time spent just browsing.
* Number of searches that returned no relevant results.
* Frequency of abandoning movies or shows midway.
* Click-through rate on recommended content.


* Collaborate with **Data Engineers** to extract raw transactional data (OLTP) and structure it into a usable Data Warehouse.


5. **Define Success Metrics**
* Establish clear evaluation metrics to track whether the model works in production.
* Compare predicted churn against actual churn and verify if the targeted discounts successfully reduced overall churn toward the 3.75% target.


6. **Choose Between Batch Learning and Online Learning**
* **Online Learning:** The model continuously learns from live streaming data. Ideal for volatile user behavior (such as lockdowns or holidays), though technically harder to implement.
* **Batch Learning:** The model is trained offline on historical data and periodically retrained (such as weekly) and redeployed when data infrastructure makes real-time learning impractical.


7. **Check and Validate Assumptions**
* **Data Availability:** Verify with data engineers that all brainstormed features are actually logged and accessible.
* **Generalization:** Question whether a single global model will perform equally well across different regions (such as the US vs. India) or if separate regional models are needed.
